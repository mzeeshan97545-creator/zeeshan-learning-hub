Module 23: Write User Experience (UX) Reports

Learning Outcomes: After completing this module, learners will be able to choose appropriate success metrics, prioritize primary use cases over edge cases, walk through user flows as first-time users, consider user expectations, evaluate usability heuristics and service structures, write comprehensive UX improvement reports, and avoid creating new bottlenecks[cite: 8].

1. Choosing Success Metrics & Tracking Drop-Offs

  • Success Metrics: Measurable values like conversion rate, click-through rate (CTR), engagement rate, bounce rate, ROI, and retention rate used to evaluate design performance[cite: 8].
  • Drop-Off Rates: Track where users abandon tasks between sequential steps (e.g., Cart to Checkout) to identify the biggest opportunities for conversion improvements[cite: 8].

2. Prioritizing Primary Use Cases & Impact

  • Common vs. Edge Cases: Give greater weight to core actions performed regularly by the majority of users rather than rare, unusual scenarios[cite: 8].
  • Cumulative Impact: Focus changes on high-traffic touchpoints to maximize speed, clarity, and overall customer satisfaction[cite: 8].

3. Walking the User Flow & Visual Documentation

  • First-Time Experience: Clear browser caches, use fresh accounts, and walk through every step from homepage to final goal like a brand-new user[cite: 8].
  • Visual Mapping: Capture screenshots after every major action and lay them out in sequence to audit user journeys and spot friction points[cite: 8].
### 4. Evaluating Usability Heuristics & Expectations
  • Heuristic Principles: Apply user control (undo options), human limitations (avoiding cognitive overload), modal integrity, linguistic clarity, and error prevention[cite: 8].
  • Onboard Help: Implement tooltips, help icons, welcome tours, and searchable FAQs to guide first-time users[cite: 8].

5. Evaluating Service Structure & Writing UX Reports

  • Service Structure: Evaluate component feature switching, cross-component data consistency (e.g., persistent shopping carts), and global notification visibility[cite: 8].
  • Improvement Reports: Structure reports with an introduction, documented findings, clear problem-solution suggestions, expected benefits, and safeguards against introducing new bottlenecks[cite: 8].
